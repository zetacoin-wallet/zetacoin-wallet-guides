## What the project will not tell you

Zetacoin's public material describes the protocol and the software. It does not publish target prices, expected returns or rankings, and it will not tell you whether to buy.

That is not evasion. A prediction has no basis in the code: block time, emission and consensus are facts, and what a market pays for them is not. Any page that converts the first into the second is guessing on your behalf.

The full walk-through, with the checks in order, is in [Zetacoin Price and Market Data](https://github.com/zetacoin-wallet/zetacoin-wallet-guides/blob/main/price-and-market-data-sources.md).

## What thin liquidity implies for a holder

The practical risk in a thin market is exit rather than entry. Buying is possible at a price you can see; selling a large position may move the price against you or take time to fill.

That asymmetry matters more than any percentage gain, because it determines whether a position can be realised at all. A limit order that sits unfilled is not a price you can transact at.

For the background behind this step, [Zetacoin Exchange List and Trading Pairs](https://github.com/zetacoin-wallet/zetacoin-wallet-guides/blob/main/exchange-list-and-trading-pairs.md) covers the same ground in more detail.

## The role emission plays

Under 50,000 new ZET a year against roughly 59.9 million in circulation is below a tenth of a percent annually. New supply is therefore not a meaningful short-term driver, which is unusual among small-cap coins and worth knowing.

It also means staking and mining are not diluting holders quickly. The trade-off is that the reward per block is small, so the incentives come from participation rather than from a large yield.

## The questions that actually decide it

Whether an asset suits you depends on time horizon, the size of the position relative to your finances, and whether you can tolerate a thin market. Those are personal, and no article can answer them.

What an article can do is remove the factual uncertainty: you know the emission, the liquidity, the listing position and the maintenance status. The decision that remains is the one only you can make.

Treat any source that offers a target price as a source with something to sell. The absence of that offer here is deliberate.

## A reasonable way to think about holding

If the reason to hold is utility  -  sending ZET, staking a balance, running a node  -  then the price is secondary and the operational questions dominate. Those have answers, and they are on the other pages here.

If the reason is return, then the relevant inputs are liquidity, horizon and your tolerance for a market that trades rarely. None of those is improved by optimism.

## Common questions

### Will ZET go up?

No one can answer that, and the project does not publish forecasts. What is known is that the market is thin and the emission is low.

### Is Zetacoin a good long-term hold?

That depends on your circumstances and your reason for holding. The protocol facts are stable and checkable; the market outcome is not predictable.

### Where can I check the price?

On the venues that list ZET, and on aggregators that cover it. On a thin market, check the venue you would actually trade on.

### Does the project recommend buying?

No. It publishes the software and the protocol facts.

## Separating the protocol from the market

The protocol is stable and documented: 30-second blocks, hybrid consensus, fixed stake reward, low annual emission, a supply of roughly 59.9 million. Those facts will be true next year regardless of what the market does.

The market is a different object. It is thin, it prices a small float, and it can move on very little trading. Reading protocol facts and inferring a market outcome is the most common reasoning error in this space, and it is the one this page refuses to make.

What a reader can do is take the two sets of facts separately and decide whether the combination suits them.

## Questions worth asking yourself

Is the amount involved one you could leave untouched for years without needing it? Can you hold your own keys, which means managing a file and a passphrase responsibly? Would a position you could not sell quickly cause you difficulty?

Those questions are about you rather than about Zetacoin, and they are the ones that decide whether an allocation makes sense. None of them is answered by a chart, and a page that answered them for you would be guessing.

## What the wallet contributes either way

Holding ZET in your own wallet removes custodial risk, which is a separate axis from market risk. A balance on an exchange is exposed to that exchange; a balance in the desktop client is exposed to your backup discipline instead.

Choosing which risk to carry is part of the decision, and {{ARTICLE:is-zetacoin-wallet-safe-to-use}} sets out the second one honestly.

## What a decision needs that a page cannot supply

A decision needs your time horizon, your other holdings, and how much of your position you could lose without changing your plans. None of those is visible from outside, and none can be inferred from the facts about a coin.

What a page can do is remove ambiguity about the asset: the chain is maintained, the emission is low, the market is thin, and the project publishes no forecast. A reader who knows those four things can decide without being misled.

Anyone who wants a number before deciding will find one somewhere, and it will be invented. The absence of that number here is the most useful thing this page offers.

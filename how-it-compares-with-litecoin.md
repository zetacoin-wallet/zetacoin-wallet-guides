Zetacoin and Litecoin are both Scrypt-based coins that began in the early altcoin era, and they differ in consensus, supply and market. Litecoin is pure Proof-of-Work with a large market; Zetacoin is hybrid Proof-of-Work and Proof-of-Stake with 30-second blocks, a ~59.9 million supply and thin liquidity. This is the one page in this pack that compares.

## Where they are similar

Both use Scrypt, which was designed to be memory-hard and therefore less dominated by specialised hardware than SHA-256 at the time. Both keep a public ledger, both are open source, and both can be mined.

Both also share a design assumption from 2011-2013: fast blocks, a fixed emission schedule, and a community-led development model rather than a company with a treasury.

| Feature | Zetacoin | Litecoin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Launched | August 2013 | October 2011 |
| Hashing | Scrypt | Scrypt |
| Consensus | hybrid PoW and PoS | Proof-of-Work |
| Block time | 30 seconds | 2.5 minutes |
| PoS reward | 0.0625 ZET | none |
| PoW reward | about 0.1 ZET | halving schedule |
| Supply | about 59.9 million ZET | 84 million cap |
| Address prefix | `9` | `L` |
| Market depth | thin | deep |

The full walk-through, with the checks in order, is in [What Is Zetacoin? The Scrypt Coin Behind the ZET Wallet](https://github.com/zetacoin-wallet/zetacoin-wallet-guides/blob/main/what-is-the-zet-coin.md).

## Where they differ, and why it matters

The largest difference is consensus. Litecoin produces blocks only through mining, so block production depends on hashrate. Zetacoin pays for Proof-of-Stake blocks as well, which means a wallet holding mature coins can produce blocks without any hardware beyond a machine that stays on.

That changes who can participate. On Litecoin, earning requires mining equipment or buying coins. On Zetacoin, holding is enough to stake, and the reward is a flat amount per block rather than a share of fees and subsidy that scales with hashrate.

The second difference is supply. Litecoin has a hard cap of 84 million reached through halvings. Zetacoin's current chain circulates about 59.9 million with under 50,000 new coins a year across both block types, which is a very different emission curve from a halving schedule.

## Block time and what it changes

A 30-second block makes a transaction confirm quickly and makes the chain longer for the same period of time. Litecoin's 2.5-minute blocks are slower to confirm and produce a shorter chain.

For a user, the practical effect is confirmation latency. For a node operator, the effect is disk and bandwidth, since more blocks means more data over the same period, even if each block is small.

For the background behind this step, [Zetacoin vs ZetaChain: Two Different Projects](https://github.com/zetacoin-wallet/zetacoin-wallet-guides/blob/main/zet-vs-zetachain-key-differences.md) covers the same ground in more detail.

## Market and liquidity

This is where the two are least comparable. Litecoin trades on major exchanges with deep order books; Zetacoin trades on smaller venues with thin books, and a moderate order can move its price.

That difference is not a judgement about either network. It is a statement about participation, and it decides what a holder can practically do with a position.

## Which fits which need

If the need is a widely accepted payment asset with deep liquidity, Zetacoin is not that, and this page will not pretend otherwise. If the need is a small, low-emission chain where holding earns blocks without hardware, Zetacoin's hybrid consensus is the reason to look at it.

If the need is to mine with Scrypt hardware at scale, Litecoin's hashrate competition is a different game from Zetacoin's, where a single machine can still find blocks.

## The honest framing

Both are legitimate early-era Scrypt coins with public ledgers. They differ in consensus, scale and market depth, and those differences are the whole comparison. Neither needs to be disparaged for the other to be useful.

## What the comparison is not

It is not a ranking, and neither coin needs to lose for the other to be useful. Both are early Scrypt coins with public ledgers, and they made different choices about consensus, supply and scale.

What follows from those choices is what a reader should take away: who can produce blocks, how supply grows, how quickly a payment settles, and how easily a position can be exited.

## Questions worth asking before choosing

Is the goal to hold and earn without hardware? Zetacoin's Proof-of-Stake half answers that, and Litecoin's does not. Is the goal a widely accepted payment asset with deep liquidity? That is Litecoin's side of the comparison.

Is the goal to mine Scrypt at scale? Both are Scrypt, and the competitive situation differs enormously between a chain with a large hashrate and one where a single machine can still find blocks.

## Common questions

### Are Zetacoin and Litecoin related?

No. They share the Scrypt algorithm and an early-era origin, and nothing else: different chains, supplies, consensus rules and markets.

### Which has faster blocks?

Zetacoin targets 30 seconds; Litecoin targets 2.5 minutes.

### Does Zetacoin have Proof-of-Stake?

Yes, hybrid Proof-of-Work and Proof-of-Stake, with 0.0625 ZET per PoS block and about 0.1 ZET per PoW block.

### Which has more liquidity?

Litecoin, by a wide margin. Zetacoin trades on smaller venues with thin order books.

## What a holder of either coin should take away

The comparison is about mechanism rather than merit. A chain where holding produces blocks has a different participant profile from one where only hashrate does, and a chain with deep liquidity offers an exit that a thin one does not.

Reading those differences correctly is more useful than any ranking, because it tells you what you would be able to do tomorrow rather than which coin "wins".

## The takeaway in one line

Two legitimate Scrypt coins with different answers to who can produce blocks and how deep the market is. Zetacoin earns from holding as well as mining; Litecoin earns only from hashrate, and its market is deep.

If that difference does not matter to what you are trying to do, the comparison has done its job by showing there is nothing else to choose between them.

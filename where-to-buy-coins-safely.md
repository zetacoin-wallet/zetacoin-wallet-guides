ZET is bought on smaller exchanges rather than major ones: it is not listed on Binance, Coinbase or Kraken. The venue list changes, so check the project's exchange page on the day you trade, confirm the pair exists, and withdraw to a `9...` address in your own wallet afterwards. Expect thin liquidity.

## The venues that actually list ZET

Trading happens on a handful of smaller platforms, and the composition shifts as exchanges add and drop pairs. Naming a stable list here would be misleading within months, which is why the project keeps an exchange page and why that page is the one to consult.

What can be stated with confidence is the negative: ZET is not on the largest exchanges. Anyone claiming a major listing is either mistaken about which coin they are looking at or describing another project with a similar name.

That distinction is worth holding onto, because the name collision with ZetaChain produces a steady stream of confusion about which venues carry which asset.

## What thin liquidity means in practice

A thin market has wide spreads and shallow order books. A modest order can move the price noticeably, and a market order can fill far from the quoted price.

The practical responses are simple: use limit orders rather than market orders, split a large purchase into smaller ones, and check the spread before deciding the price is acceptable. None of that is specific to ZET; it is how any small-cap pair behaves.

Thin liquidity also means the quoted price on an aggregator may not be achievable in size. Treat an aggregator figure as an indication rather than a promise.

## Buying, step by step

1. Choose a venue from the project's exchange page and create an account there.
2. Complete whatever verification the venue requires.
3. Deposit the currency for the pair you intend to trade.
4. Place a limit order rather than a market order.
5. Withdraw the ZET to a wallet you control, to an address beginning with `9`.

The last step is the one that matters most. Coins left on an exchange are a claim on the exchange rather than coins you hold, and small venues are exactly where that distinction becomes real.

For the background behind this step, [Move Zetacoin Out of an Exchange](https://github.com/zetacoin-wallet/zetacoin-wallet-guides/blob/main/move-coins-out-of-an-exchange.md) covers the same ground in more detail.

## Withdrawing to your own wallet

Withdrawal requires a Zetacoin address. Generate it in your wallet and check the first character is a nine; an address starting with Z belongs to the retired chain and cannot receive current ZET.

Send a small test amount first if the venue is unfamiliar. It costs a little and it proves the address, the network and the venue's withdrawal process all work before a larger amount is at stake.

Then confirm the balance in the wallet after the exchange's confirmation threshold is met, which may be slower than the chain itself.

## What to check before trusting a venue

The usual checks apply: whether the venue has a working withdrawal process, whether it has a history longer than a few months, and whether its ZET pair actually trades rather than merely existing.

A pair with no volume is a listing in name only. It cannot be sold into, which means the price shown is decorative.

For a coin of this size, the strongest signal is that the project's own exchange page names the venue. That is not an endorsement of its safety, but it is at least evidence the pair is real.

## Selling and moving on

Selling follows the same path in reverse, with the same liquidity caveats. A large sale into a thin book moves the price against you, and a limit order may sit unfilled for a long time.

If the intention is to convert to another asset, the pair available decides the route. ZET is typically paired against Bitcoin or a stablecoin, which means two trades rather than one.

## Common questions

### Can I buy ZET on Binance or Coinbase?

No. ZET is not listed on those exchanges, and claims otherwise usually refer to a different project.

### Why is the price different on different sites?

Thin markets and different pairs produce different quotes. An aggregator figure is an indication, not a tradable price in size.

### Should I leave coins on the exchange?

No. Withdraw to a wallet you control, to a `9...` address, once you have confirmed the venue's withdrawal process works.

### How do I know a venue is legitimate?

There is no guarantee. Check that withdrawals work, that the pair trades, and that the project's exchange page names it; then withdraw promptly rather than leaving a balance behind.

## The short version

Smaller venues, thin books, limit orders, and withdrawal to your own wallet. Verify the pair is live and the address starts with nine.

## The cost of buying in two steps

Most purchases begin in fiat and end in ZET, which means acquiring a quote currency and then trading it. Each step has a spread and a fee, and on a thin pair the spread is the larger of the two.

Adding them up before deciding the price is reasonable is the difference between a purchase that feels cheap and one that was. On a small market, the route matters as much as the quote.

## What to do with the coins after purchase

Withdraw them. A purchase that stays on the venue has changed a fiat balance into an exchange balance, which is not the same as owning ZET.

The withdrawal needs a `9...` address from your own wallet, and a small test first is worth the fee when the venue is unfamiliar. Once the coins arrive, back up the wallet file and encrypt it if it is not already encrypted.

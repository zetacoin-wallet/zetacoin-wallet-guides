ZET trades on a small number of venues, not on the major exchanges. Komodo Wallet is the venue the project's own sources agree on, alongside others that change over time. Trading pairs are typically against Bitcoin or a stablecoin, and liquidity is thin on all of them. Check the project's exchange page before trading.

## Why this list is short and changes

A coin of this size has few venues, and the ones that list it come and go. Exchanges add pairs when there is demand and drop them when there is not, and a list copied into an article becomes wrong faster than it becomes useful.

The project maintains an exchange page for exactly this reason. It is the only place where the list is maintained by the people who have a reason to keep it current.

What follows is therefore about how to evaluate a venue rather than a permanent directory.

| Consideration | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Pair exists | a live ZET market, not an empty listing |
| Withdrawals work | a documented, functioning withdrawal process |
| Venue age | longer history is weaker evidence of safety, but absence of one is a warning |
| Project's page | the venue is named where the project tracks venues |
| Liquidity | a spread you can live with at the size you intend |

The full walk-through, with the checks in order, is in [Where to Buy Zetacoin](https://github.com/zetacoin-wallet/zetacoin-wallet-guides/blob/main/where-to-buy-coins-safely.md).

## Komodo Wallet and self-custody venues

Komodo Wallet appears consistently across the project's own descriptions of where ZET can be held or traded, which is why it is the one venue worth naming here.

Self-custody platforms are a different category from a centralised exchange: you hold the keys, the swap happens against a counterparty, and there is no account balance sitting on someone else's books. That removes custody risk and adds the requirement that you understand what you are signing.

For a small-cap coin, that trade is often the right one, because the failure mode of a small centralised venue  -  withdrawal freezes  -  is the one that costs people everything.

## Pairs and what they imply

ZET is usually paired against Bitcoin or a stablecoin rather than against fiat. That means a purchase in fiat involves two steps: acquire the quote currency, then trade it for ZET.

Two steps means two spreads and two sets of fees. On a thin market, that is a meaningful part of the cost, and it is why comparing the total route matters more than comparing the headline ZET price.

For the background behind this step, [Move Zetacoin Out of an Exchange](https://github.com/zetacoin-wallet/zetacoin-wallet-guides/blob/main/move-coins-out-of-an-exchange.md) covers the same ground in more detail.

## What the absent listings tell you

ZET is not on Binance, Coinbase or Kraken. That is not a scandal; it is the normal position of a small, long-running coin that has not pursued large-exchange listings.

What it does mean is that anyone describing ZET as available on a major exchange is either confused about the ticker or talking about another project. The name collision with ZetaChain produces this error constantly.

## Checking a venue before you use it

Start from the project's exchange page rather than from a search result. Then verify that the pair is live, that the order book has depth at a plausible price, and that the withdrawal process is documented and working.

Test with a small amount. A withdrawal of a small balance proves the whole path: deposit, trade, withdrawal, confirmation in your own wallet. That test costs a little and prevents the situation where a balance is stranded on a venue that cannot send it.

## Withdrawing after purchase

Send ZET to an address from your own wallet, and confirm it begins with `9`. A `Z...` address belongs to the retired chain and cannot receive current coins.

Allow for the venue's confirmation threshold, which may be higher than the chain's own settlement. If the withdrawal does not appear, check a public explorer before assuming the venue has failed.

## Common questions

### Is ZET on any major exchange?

No. It is not listed on Binance, Coinbase or Kraken.

### Which exchange is the most reliable?

No venue carries a guarantee. Komodo Wallet is the one the project's sources agree on; for any other, verify the pair and test a withdrawal.

### What pairs does ZET trade against?

Typically Bitcoin or a stablecoin. Fiat purchases therefore involve two trades.

### Why is the list different on other sites?

Because listings change and aggregators lag. The project's own page is the one maintained by people with a reason to keep it accurate.

Few venues, thin books, Bitcoin and stablecoin pairs, and Komodo Wallet as the consistent name. Verify before depositing and withdraw promptly.

## How a venue list goes wrong

Lists go stale in a predictable order. A venue stops accepting deposits, then stops processing withdrawals, then disappears from comparison sites, while the article that named it stays online and keeps sending people to it.

That sequence is why a maintained page beats a copied list, and why the practical skill is evaluating a venue rather than memorising names.

## Judging a venue in ten minutes

Check that the pair has recent trades, that the order book has depth near the quoted price, and that the withdrawal page documents a process. Then test with a small amount before committing a larger one.

If any of those checks fails, the venue is not usable regardless of what it lists. A pair that shows a price with no trades is a display, not a market, and a withdrawal process that cannot be tested is a promise rather than a feature.

{{ARTICLE:zetacoin-price-and-market-data}} covers what a quoted price means on a thin book, and {{ARTICLE:move-zetacoin-out-of-exchange}} covers the withdrawal that follows a purchase.

## What to do when a venue disappears

If a venue stops processing withdrawals, stop depositing and start documenting: screenshots of the balance, the withdrawal request and any support response. Then look for an official statement from the venue rather than a rumour.

The practical lesson is the one already stated: balances left on small venues are exposed, and the exposure is realised exactly when withdrawal stops working. A holding in your own wallet is exposed to your backup discipline instead, which is a risk you can actually manage.
